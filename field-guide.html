macOS · Homebrew mpv

mpv Field Guide

Everything your setup does, and which key does it.

01 Start watching

There is nothing to launch. mpv is the default player for every common video type.

  1. Double-click any video in Finder.

    It opens in mpv. Every other video in that same folder is queued behind it automatically — a 1,200-file folder loads in about a sixth of a second. Images, subtitles and other clutter are filtered out.

  2. Or start from the folder itself.

    Right-click the folder → Open With → mpv, or drag it onto the mpv icon. Same result, played in order. A plain double-click on a folder always opens Finder — macOS reserves that.

  3. Open more videos whenever you like.

    A second video never opens a second window. It joins the queue and starts playing, and the first one stays available behind it.

  4. Close it whenever. Just close it.

    Your position is saved automatically. Open that file again — today or next month — and it picks up exactly where you stopped.

02 Moving inside a video

Four fixed jump sizes, and two more that work out their own landing point. Every one lands on the exact second — none of them drift to the nearest keyframe. Below them, three keys change how fast the video runs rather than where you are in it.

Shift+← →
Jump a chapter back or forward. Most films only mark a few act breaks, but a WEB-DL episode often marks every scene — those are the little triangles along the seek bar
/
Skip the opening titles. Runs the video forward at speed until the sound drops away — which on an episode is the pause between the title sequence and the first line. Unlike the chapter key above it needs no chapter marks, so it works on the many files that carry none. Press it again while it is running to call it off and go back to where you were, and if the sound never drops it gives up on its own rather than running to the end
a d
3 seconds back and forward — for catching a line of dialogue
→ ←
10 seconds forward, 6 seconds back — the everyday nudge. Deliberately lopsided: forward skips past a dull stretch, back replays a line you missed
⌘+→ ←
30 seconds forward, 10 seconds back — the same nudge one size up, and lopsided for the same reason. Forward now reads as a ladder: right arrow 10 seconds, add ⌘ for 30, add Option for a minute
Option+← →
1 minute — the blunt instrument, for a long stretch you want gone. Either Option key works
] [
Faster and slower, a tenth at a time. Voices keep their proper pitch instead of turning into chipmunks, so a slow stretch at 1.3x is still perfectly listenable
} {
Double and half speed — the big jump, for skimming a video to find a moment rather than watching it. Shift and the same two keys
Delete
Back to normal speed. The Delete key above Return, not the one in the arrow cluster
Space
Pause and resume
Speed is a “right now” choice — it never follows you around. Whatever you were watching at double speed, the next video you open plays at normal speed. The one exception is deliberate: when an episode ends and the next starts by itself, it keeps the speed you set, so a run through a season at 2x isn't interrupted every 40 minutes. Left to itself mpv does the opposite — it files the speed away with your saved position, so a show watched once at 4x reopens at 4x for good.

03 Moving between videos

The queue is the folder you opened from, so this works without selecting anything.

x
Next video
z
Previous video. While shuffle is on it is a proper back button — it returns to the video you actually watched last, not the one sitting before this in folder order
⌘+z
Shuffle on/off. While on, both x and a video ending pick at random, and z retraces what you have watched. Nothing repeats until the whole folder has played
Re-clicking a video you already have open does not restart it. It returns to where you were, lifts the pause, and doesn't add a duplicate to the queue.

04 Where is this file?

For when you want the file itself — to rename it, move it, take a piece out of it, or just see where it came from.

⌘+Delete
Move this video to the Trash. The same chord Finder uses. Playback carries straight on from where it was and mpv never quits — the video keeps playing from a file that is already in the Trash, and the dead entry is dropped from the queue once you leave it
⌘+t
Save a piece of this video. Mark where the bit you want starts, mark where it ends, and it writes just that stretch to the Desktop as an ordinary mp4 — the kind anything will play, including a phone. The clip keeps the subtitles and picture settings you had on at the time. A few seconds long takes a few seconds to make
i
Show in Finder. Opens the folder the video lives in, in a new Finder window, with the file already selected — exactly as if you had clicked it there
From fullscreen on the laptop screen this switches you to Finder's Space, because that kind of fullscreen is a Space of its own. Press p first if you would rather stay put.

05 Subtitles, sound, screen

c
Subtitles. Off until you ask. One press turns them on with the best English subtitles the video has. Each press after that moves to the next one — any other English track first, then Turkish — and then off, with the message on screen telling you what the next press will do. A video with one track of one language is simply on, then off. It picks the best track, not the first one: a film can carry thirty-five subtitle files, and it reads their names, their language tags and the film's own filename to find the real dialogue. It now also ranks a Signs & Songs track below the real subtitles — that kind of track captions on-screen text and song lyrics but not one word of the dialogue, and Attack on Titan ships one right alongside its full subtitles, which is what used to get picked
b
Download subtitles. When the film has none, or none worth watching. It searches the subtitle sites for this exact rip, in English and in Turkish — it fingerprints the file rather than going by the title — saves what it finds next to the video, and switches it on. A film takes a few seconds; an episode can take a minute, and it counts up while it looks. Shift+b fetches a fresh copy over one you already have
alt+b
Fix subtitle timing automatically. For a subtitle that is the right words at the wrong moment — usually a download timed for a different cut. It listens to the film, works out where the speech actually is, and rewrites the subtitle to match, so the fix is permanent. Takes a couple of minutes on a long film, counting up as it goes. The untouched original is kept alongside, and this only works on a downloaded subtitle, not a built-in one
alt+c
Choose a subtitle track from the full list — for a second language, or when the chosen one isn't the one you wanted. Searchable; arrow keys and Return
C X
Subtitle timing, in tenths of a second. C holds the subtitles back, X pulls them earlier. For a subtitle that is close but drifting against the speech
v
Cancel subtitles. Switches them off wherever you are in the cycle, so the next c starts fresh in English
l
Change the spoken language. A film with more than one soundtrack starts in English without being asked — an anime with an English dub plays dubbed, not subtitled. One press moves to the next language the film carries, another comes back round to English, and the message on screen names the language you have just moved to and the one the next press will give. It picks the film's real soundtrack, not a director's commentary that happens to be in English and happens to be listed first
alt+l
Choose a soundtrack from the full list — a second English mix, or that commentary. Searchable, like the subtitle list above. l deals in languages, so on a film with two English mixes it offers English once; this is how you reach the other one
↑ ↓  or  w s
Volume, up and down. Two pairs that do exactly the same thing — the arrows, or w and s for one hand off the arrow keys. Either shows a vertical bar on the right, with a tick marking 100%. The level is shared by everything — set it once and the next video opens at the same volume, instead of each file remembering its own
n
Even out the volume. For a film where the dialogue is a whisper and the action is deafening — it lifts the quiet passages and holds the loud ones down. Press again to switch it off. The screen now says which way it went: this key sits right next to the subtitle-download key, and a missed press used to change the sound with nothing at all to show for it
f
Fullscreen. Opens in its own Space on the laptop screen, and simply covers the desktop on an external display
p
Switch how fullscreen works. This is chosen for you — its own Space on the laptop screen, and plain desktop-covering on a TV or external monitor. Press p to overrule it until you change screens again
g
Zoom to fill. A wide film normally sits between two black bands. This crops them away so the picture fills the screen — at the cost of losing a strip off each side. Press again to go back
y
Sharper picture. Cartoons and anime are cleaned up while they play — the lines come back crisp instead of soft, because a 1080p video is being stretched a long way to fill this screen. It decides on its own which videos get it: animated shows do, live action does not. This key overrules that decision — one press forces it on for whatever you are watching, the next turns it off, the third hands the choice back. The message says which of the three you have landed on, and how strong the sharpening is at that moment — see section 08. It never alters the video file, only what reaches the screen, so switching it off puts the picture back exactly as it was
Shift+I
Playback statistics, if you ever suspect stutter. This was on plain i until Show in Finder took that key; mpv offers the page on both, and this one stays up until you press it again

06 Saved positions

Every video remembers where you stopped — when you close mpv, and also when you jump to another video with x, z or shuffle. Three things clear that memory.

  • Finishing a video clears its own position, so it starts fresh next time.
  • Thirty days — anything untouched that long is dropped automatically at startup.
  • 0 then 0 again clears the videos you have open — the folder you are watching, any other folder you opened alongside it, and any folder inside those — and sends the video on screen back to its own start. Everything outside that keeps its place. The first press only arms it and tells you how many videos it covers; the second commits. The arming lapses after 5 seconds, so two stray presses minutes apart can't do it, and a second press that arrives too late says so instead of silently starting over.
Close the playlist menu before you press it. While mpv's own playlist list is open (Ctrl+p) it claims every ordinary key for its search box, so 0 is typed into the filter and never reaches the reset. Nothing is cleared and the video keeps resuming — which looks exactly like a reset that did nothing.
Positions are keyed to the file's path, so renaming or moving a video loses its place.

07 Worth knowing

  • mpv’s own volume keys are mostly still there. *, the numpad ÷ and ×, and the mouse wheel all still change volume — but in steps of 2, and they show mpv’s flat horizontal bar instead of the vertical one. 9 was switched off, and /, which used to be one of these, now skips the titles instead — so on the main keyboard volume down is the arrows or s.
  • Shuffle history resets when you quit. A fresh session can replay something you saw in the last one.
  • Back and forward behave like a browser while shuffling. z retraces the videos you actually watched, x walks that trail forward again, and only once you are back at the newest one does x pick a fresh random video.
  • Subtitles never return on their own. Even if you had them on when you quit, reopening starts clean.
  • The window never opens bigger than 90% of the screen. Left to itself mpv sizes the window to the video, and a 1080p file on a 1080p screen gives a window exactly as large as the display — so f appears to do nothing. Capping it keeps fullscreen visibly different.
  • On a TV, fullscreen covers the desktop rather than taking its own Space. The trade is a faint hairline at the screen edge, which nobody sees from a sofa. p switches it either way.
  • Moving the mouse shows a slim progress bar, not the full control bar. The controls — buttons, filename, timers — only appear when you push the pointer right down to the bottom edge of the screen, and they go away again after two seconds of a stationary mouse. Left to itself mpv keeps them up indefinitely while the pointer rests on them.
  • The elapsed time sits under the bar, at the bottom of the screen. Short messages like “Subtitles off” or “Zoom to fill” still appear in the top-left corner, where mpv puts them.
  • Zoom is remembered per film. Unlike subtitles, a film you zoomed with g is still zoomed when you come back to it.
  • Speed is not remembered — the opposite of zoom, and on purpose. Every video you open starts at normal speed; only a playlist moving on by itself carries the speed across. See section 02.
  • Shift+Q saves and quits — the same as closing normally, kept because it's an mpv habit.
  • Return, > and < are dead too. All three were spare copies of x and z, and a stray press skipped the video. The mouse’s back and forward buttons still do it.
  • l used to set loop points, and then to show the file in Finder. mpv's default l marked the start and end of a repeating section, which nothing here ever used; it was Show in Finder for a week, and is now the audio-language key. Show in Finder moved to i.
  • mpv has no language preference of its own. It plays whichever soundtrack the file marks as its default, whatever language that is — on Solo Leveling season 2 that is Japanese, every episode. Preferring English is a setting this config adds, and the two cases it cannot cover on its own — a soundtrack labelled "English" but tagged with no language at all, and an English commentary listed ahead of the film — are handled by a script.
  • w and s used to do something else. By default they nudged the crop and took a screenshot; both were easy to hit by accident, and both are gone. Cropping has a proper on/off switch on g instead.

08 Running under the hood

Things you never press, doing work while you watch.

  • Hardware decoding. Playback runs on the Mac's dedicated video decoder rather than the CPU — measured 19.6% → 4.9% CPU on 1080p. Quieter, cooler, longer battery, and the picture is identical.
  • High-quality upscaling. A sharper scaler for 720p and 1080p on a Retina screen. This one costs GPU, and it's the deliberate trade for a better picture.
  • Extra sharpening for animation. On top of that, animated shows go through Anime4K, which redraws the line art rather than merely enlarging it — y in section 05 is the switch. Live action is left out on purpose, because the same treatment makes real skin look waxy.
  • The sharpening follows the machine, not the cable. The full treatment needs more graphics power than this Mac reliably has spare — and how much is spare changes by the minute. Plugging in is itself what sets macOS off on the background work it saves up for the wall socket, so being on the charger is often when there is least going spare, not most: caught on 20 September 2026 with no video playing at all, the photo analyser had a processor core pinned for 41 hours straight and a quarter of the graphics chip was already spoken for. So there are now three settings — the full treatment, a lighter one, and off — and it moves between them by watching whether the picture is actually keeping up. Lose more than a fiftieth of the frames and it steps down at once; go a clear minute without losing any and it climbs back up one. Unplugged it stays off, because that work comes straight out of the battery for a picture you did not ask for, and y turns it back on by hand. It says so on screen whenever it moves — a picture that changes by itself should not look like a fault. It deliberately does not go and ask the system anything while the sound is playing: that kind of check used to make the audio skip, which is heard as a thump, so this one only reads numbers mpv is already keeping. An earlier version of this page called the full treatment free; that was measured plugged in, on a quiet machine.
  • On battery it also stops drawing frames you cannot see. This screen refreshes 120 times a second; a film has only 24 new frames a second. Keeping step with the screen rather than the film means four out of every five trips through the whole picture pipeline redraw a frame that is already on screen. On battery it keeps step with the film instead — about half the graphics work, for a picture that is identical pixel for pixel. The cost is a very slight hitch roughly every 8 seconds, which is the thing keeping step with the screen was there to remove. On the charger, nothing changes.
  • One window, always. Selecting a whole folder and opening it can't spawn a second player — the launcher takes a lock so only one instance is ever created.
  • Thumbnails for MKV and WebM, via QuickLook Video. macOS can't preview those formats on its own.

09 Where it all lives

Every setting is commented in place with the reason it's there.

PathWhat's in it
~/.config/mpv/mpv.confSettings — subtitles, seek bar, fullscreen, resume, folder queueing
~/.config/mpv/input.confEvery keybinding on this page, and a note for the one bound elsewhere
~/.config/mpv/mpv-openThe one-window launcher. Lives outside the app on purpose
~/.config/mpv/scripts/Vertical volume bar, shuffle mode, fullscreen mode, mouse progress bar, seek thumbnails, control-bar idle hide, show in Finder, move to Trash, one global volume, audio-language choosing, subtitle choosing, downloading and re-timing, position expiry and reset, skipping the titles, saving a clip, sharpening animation
~/.config/mpv/shaders/The Anime4K picture filters, used only on animated shows
~/.config/mpv/script-opts/Settings for the scripts — including which folders count as animation, and how the sharpening picks its setting
~/.config/mpv/watch_later/One small file per remembered position
/Applications/mpv.appThe wrapper Finder opens, plus the purple document icons
Editing anything inside mpv.app breaks its signature, and re-signing makes macOS ask for Desktop access again. That's why the launcher and scripts sit outside it — they can be changed freely.
